# Formal & Professional Voice

> Reusable style guide for CVs, cover letters, LinkedIn, professional correspondence, and formal requests.
> Paste into any AI assistant or share with anyone drafting on Dan's behalf.

*Built from Dan's CV, formal emails, and professional communications. Edit any rule below—what Dan asks for in a task always wins over this file.*

---

## 1. Core voice in one line

Clear, confident, and human. Professional without being stiff. Gets the point across efficiently, backs claims with evidence, and sounds like a real person—not a template.

## 2. When to use this voice

| Context | Use formal voice |
|---|---|
| CV / resume | ✓ |
| Cover letter | ✓ |
| LinkedIn profile / summary | ✓ |
| Professional emails to senior leaders, HR, external parties | ✓ |
| Formal requests (leave, approvals, compliance) | ✓ |
| Reference requests | ✓ |

For portfolio copy, journal posts, and personal site content, see `TONE_PORTFOLIO.md`.

## 3. Structure and length

**CVs and profiles:** Bullet points. Start each with an active verb. One accomplishment per bullet.

**Cover letters:** Three paragraphs max. Opening (why this role), middle (evidence of fit), close (next step).

**Formal emails:** Greeting → purpose in the first line → brief context if needed → sign-off with full signature.

| Content | Length |
|---|---|
| CV summary | 3–4 sentences |
| Cover letter | 250–350 words |
| LinkedIn summary | 150–250 words |
| Formal email | Under 150 words unless detail is required |

## 4. Openings

**CV summary:** Lead with current role and the work, then the approach.

> "Senior Product Designer with experience shaping and delivering complex digital products in financial services."

**Cover letter:** State the role and why it matters to you—no "I am writing to apply for..."

| ✓ Good | ✗ Avoid |
|---|---|
| "The opportunity to shape [X] at [Company] aligns with..." | "I am writing to express my interest in..." |
| "I've spent the past four years at Commonwealth Bank..." | "Please find attached my application for..." |

**Formal email:** "Hi [Name]," then state the request or purpose immediately.

> "Hi Sarah, I would like to request annual leave from 15–22 December."

## 5. Closings and sign-offs

| Context | Sign-off |
|---|---|
| CV | No sign-off needed |
| Cover letter | "Best regards," or "Kind regards," + full name |
| Formal email | "Best," + full signature (name, title, team) |
| LinkedIn message | "Best, Dan" or "Thanks, Dan" |

**Full signature format:**
```
Best,
Daniel Shires
Senior Product Designer, Customer, Digital & AI
```

Note: Signs as **Daniel** (not Dan) in formal letters, cover letters, and when using the full signature. Signs as **Dan** everywhere else.

## 6. Active verbs for accomplishments

Start every bullet with an action verb. Avoid "Responsible for..."

| ✓ Use | ✗ Avoid |
|---|---|
| Led, shaped, delivered, designed | Was responsible for |
| Contributed to, facilitated, translated | Helped with |
| Developed, evolved, collaborated | Assisted in |
| Mentored, influenced, presented | Participated in |

**Strong examples:**
- "Led discovery into the future of customer communications, mapping current-state experiences and identifying opportunities."
- "Contributed to 1.9 million unique customer engagements between May 2024 and May 2025."
- "Mentored graduate designers, both of whom subsequently secured permanent roles."

## 7. Quantify where possible

Numbers add credibility. Include them when you have them.

| ✓ With evidence | ✗ Without |
|---|---|
| "700% increase in hub visits" | "Significant increase in engagement" |
| "More than 10 client interviews" | "Conducted interviews with clients" |
| "1.9 million unique customer engagements" | "Drove strong customer engagement" |

If you don't have exact numbers, use reasonable qualifiers: "more than", "approximately", "over".

## 8. Vocabulary — favour

**Plain professional language:** shape, deliver, contribute, collaborate, influence, present.

**Domain terms (where appropriate):** product discovery, customer-centred design, systems thinking, design systems, accessibility, concept development.

**Evidence phrases:** "contributed to", "alongside reported increases of", "with a track record of".

**Soft confidence:** "Experienced in...", "Combines...", "Increasingly integrating..."

**Australian/British spelling:** colour, behaviour, organise, centre, favour.

## 9. Vocabulary — avoid

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Passionate about" | Overused, vague |
| "Detail-oriented" | Show it, don't say it |
| "Team player" | Everyone says this |
| "Proven track record" without evidence | Hollow claim |
| "Leverage", "synergy", "utilize" | Corporate filler |
| "Kindly", "please be advised" | Stiff |
| "I hope this email finds you well" | Preamble |
| American spellings | Inconsistent |

## 10. Tone calibration

Even in formal contexts, sound human. Don't over-formalise.

| ✓ Human | ✗ Stiff |
|---|---|
| "I would like to request..." | "I hereby wish to formally request..." |
| "Happy to discuss further." | "Please do not hesitate to contact me should you require further information." |
| "Let me know if you need anything else." | "Kindly advise if additional materials are required." |

## 11. How Dan handles requests and pushback (formal contexts)

**Requests:** State what you need, give the reason briefly, offer a fallback.

> "I would like to request annual leave from 15–22 December to spend time with family. I've confirmed coverage with the team and can adjust dates if needed."

**Pushback:** Lead with a question or evidence, propose an alternative.

> "Just checking—is there flexibility on the timeline? My recommendation would be to prioritise [X] first, which would allow us to [benefit]. Happy to discuss."

Never sarcastic, never blaming. Keep the door open.

## 12. Formal email structure

```
Hi [Name],

[Purpose in one line.]

[Brief context if needed—1-2 sentences max.]

[Open door or next step.]

Best,
Daniel Shires
Senior Product Designer, Customer, Digital & AI
```

**Example:**

> Hi Sarah,
>
> I would like to request annual leave from Monday 15 December to Friday 22 December.
>
> I've confirmed coverage with Mark and the team, and I'll ensure any outstanding work is handed over before I leave.
>
> Please let me know if you need any additional information.
>
> Best,
> Daniel Shires
> Senior Product Designer, Customer, Digital & AI

## 13. Drafting checklist

Run silently before showing a draft:

- [ ] Opens with the point—no "I am writing to..."
- [ ] Active verbs start each accomplishment
- [ ] Evidence and numbers where available
- [ ] Under word limit for the format
- [ ] No corporate filler ("leverage", "synergy", "passionate")
- [ ] British/Australian spelling throughout
- [ ] Sign-off matches the context (Dan vs Daniel)
- [ ] Human tone—not robotic or overly formal
- [ ] Facts not known → placeholder like [date], never invented
